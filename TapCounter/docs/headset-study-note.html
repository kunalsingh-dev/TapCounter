Headset buttons on Android

How a press on your earbuds becomes a count, why it sometimes stops, and how the gesture timing works.

1. AVRCP: what the headset actually says

AVRCP (Audio/Video Remote Control Profile) is the Bluetooth profile for transport control. The headset is the controller, the phone is the target. A button press becomes a small pass-through command (play, pause, forward, backward, stop). The classic call button comes over HFP/HSP instead and arrives as the "hook" key.

The Android Bluetooth stack converts these commands into ordinary media KeyEvents and hands them to the system's media-key dispatcher. It does not send them to apps directly.

Volume is different. With AVRCP absolute volume (1.4+), the headset changes its own gain and only informs the phone. On most Bluetooth headsets no volume key event exists to capture. Volume buttons cannot be used for counting.

2. MediaSession: who receives the key

The system dispatcher gives a media key to one active MediaSession: in practice the one that most recently became active or reported STATE_PLAYING. Apps don't "listen globally". They hold a session. Our HeadsetSessionManager creates a MediaSessionCompat, sets a PlaybackState of PLAYING, and calls isActive = true. Keys then arrive in onMediaButtonEvent(Intent).

The session lives in a foreground service (type connectedDevice) so the process survives screen-off and backgrounding.

3. KeyEvent codes

KeyCodeTypical source
KEYCODE_HEADSETHOOK79Wired/HFP call button
KEYCODE_MEDIA_PLAY_PAUSE85Most earbuds, centre button
KEYCODE_MEDIA_STOP86Some headsets
KEYCODE_MEDIA_NEXT87Double tap on many earbuds
KEYCODE_MEDIA_PREVIOUS88Triple tap on many earbuds
KEYCODE_MEDIA_PLAY / PAUSE126 / 127Headsets with separate play and pause

Each press is ACTION_DOWN then ACTION_UP. Holding may add repeated DOWNs with repeatCount > 0. Some headsets send a duplicate DOWN, which is why we debounce. Many earbuds turn their own double/triple tap into NEXT/PREVIOUS before sending anything. That is why Learn mode shows what your headset really sends.

4. Audio focus (and why we don't use it)

Audio focus is the polite protocol for who may play sound now. It is separate from media-key routing, but the two are tied together in practice because the app that last played audio tends to win the keys. If we requested focus, other players would pause or duck. So the keep-alive track plays without requesting focus: your music is untouched. The price: a music app that starts later can still become the preferred session.

5. Why events stop arriving

  • Another app's session became active/playing more recently (music, video, a call).
  • Our session was released or deactivated, or the process was killed by a battery saver.
  • Nothing is playing and the session looks idle, so some systems deprioritise it. Hence the keep-alive loop.
  • A phone call: HFP takes the hook key for call control.
  • The headset reconnected and routing was rebuilt. We re-create the session ≈ 1.5 s after ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED.
  • The headset itself keeps the gesture (assistant, noise control) and never sends it.

Recovery tools: Re-take control (new session + restart keep-alive), ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY handling (pause keep-alive when the headset vanishes), and ACL connect/disconnect receivers.

6. Gesture timing algorithm (C++ core)

The engine never reads a clock and never sleeps. Kotlin passes timestamps and calls tick(now) at nextDeadlineMs().

onKey(key, down, t):
  tick(t)                          # resolve anything already expired
  if down:
      if key already down:            ignore            # auto-repeat
      if t - lastDown[key] < debounce: ignore            # duplicate DOWN
      state = DOWN; downTime = t
  else:
      if not down: ignore                                # orphan UP
      if longFired: clear; return                        # release after long press
      taps++; lastUp = t
      if taps == 3: emit TRIPLE; taps = 0

tick(now):                          # per key
  if down and not longFired and now - downTime >= longPress:
      flush pending taps first; emit LONG_PRESS; longFired = true
  elif up and taps > 0 and now - lastUp >= multiTapWindow:
      emit SINGLE/DOUBLE by taps; taps = 0

nextDeadline = min( downTime + longPress   if held,
                    lastUp + multiTapWindow if taps pending )

Debounce applies to DOWN events only: a duplicate DOWN is dropped, and its UP is then ignored because no DOWN was accepted. Each key has its own state, so two buttons never interfere.

Consequence: a single click is reported only after the multi-click window. That is the unavoidable price of telling single from double.

7. Reliable vs unreliable

Reliable: Play/Pause (or hook), Next, Previous, usually Stop. Unreliable: long press, anything the headset handles internally, volume up/down, any gesture while another app owns the session.
